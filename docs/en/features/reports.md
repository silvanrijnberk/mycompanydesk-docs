# Reports

Reports are built around plain questions. Every page opens with a question you ask about your business, answers it with one amount and a sentence of context, and places the action that helps next right in the answer. Charts sit inside the answers instead of beside them, and the numbers explain their own window: what is counted, and with what it is compared.

## Accessing reports

Navigate to **Reports** in the sidebar. On desktop, switch pages with the tab bar at the top; on mobile, use the page selector or swipe left and right between pages. A **Filters** button sits at the end of the tab bar and opens the period panel.

Most pages follow the period you picked. Four answer a question about right now and hide the period switch on purpose, because a year chip above them would promise a choice that does nothing: **Quotes**, **Still to pay** and **Gone quiet** under Clients, and **What's coming** under Cash flow. Deep links carry their period with them: a dashboard tile for profit or revenue opens reports on the running period of that kind (this month, this quarter, or this year).

## Overview

The overview opens with the period's revenue against last year, then answers the questions that span the whole business, one card per question:

- **What did I earn?** The profit before tax for the period: revenue minus costs, with the same period last year put next to it (higher, lower, times as high, or level). The chart under the answer shows the revenue of both years, month by month, with the still-running months marked. A link opens the sales page.
- **What should I set aside?** Shown while there is anything to set aside. The card names the VAT still to pay with its quarter and due date, estimates the income tax for the year (or the corporate tax when your company is a BV), and marks everything as a preliminary estimate your accountant works out in the end. When the hours criterion is not settled yet, a row links to the hours page: if you do not meet it, you pay more.
- **Who still owes me money?** The outstanding total and how many invoices it is spread over, the client who has kept you waiting longest, the late invoices with their day counts, and a button that opens the overdue invoices. When everyone paid, the card says "Nobody. All invoices have been paid." When invoices are open but none are late, it says so.
- **Who do I rely on?** Your biggest client with their share of your revenue, whether that is a lot to lean on one client or a healthy spread, and where your growth comes from: when almost all of it comes from this client, the card says what your revenue would have done without them. A share bar visualises the spread.
- **Am I leaving money on the table?** Only shown when something is unclaimed: tracked hours not invoiced yet (put them on an invoice and the money comes in) and quotes that expired without an answer from the client.
- **Want to know more?** A link grid into the other pages, ending with "How much tax do I pay?" toward the tax section.

A **Change period** button sits at the top, next to the period label.

## Sales

**Sales** answers "What do I sell?" with revenue over time, revenue per service or product from your Catalog, the best-selling items with units, share and average sale value, and the services-versus-products split. Invoices link to Catalog items for this page; items without a link show as unlinked, with a note that linking makes the page exact.

The questions under the numbers all compare the period with the same period last year over an equally long window: **How are my sales doing?**, **Where does that growth come from?** (naming the client behind the growth when it is mostly one of them), **Where is the drop?**, and **Am I selling bigger jobs?** with the average invoice of now against the average of last year.

## Quotes

**Quotes** is a snapshot of today. **What's still waiting for an answer?** names the outstanding value and how many quotes it is spread over, counts how many have already expired without a yes or no, and lets you extend a quote's validity or open all quotes; quotes still valid are still within the client's answering time. **How many quotes turn into a job?** answers with the wins out of the quotes that got decided: an expired quote counts as a no, and the won quotes carry their combined value of work. **Can I quote higher?** weighs the size of your quotes against what they turn into.

## Clients

**Clients** opens on three tabs: **Buyers**, **Still to pay** and **Gone quiet**.

- **Buyers** follows the picked period against the same period last year. It answers: **Who is growing and who is shrinking?** (your biggest clients next to what they bought in the same period a year earlier, with new clients named and level results marked), **Who is going quiet?** (regular customers who now stay away much longer than their own habit; under each name a bar draws their usual gap as a line and the current gap as a bar, with the ones furthest past their habit on top, and an action to send a message or check a quote that is still out), and **Who pays late?** (paid invoices from this period, the average days past the due date per client, and one-time late payers against habitual ones).
- **Still to pay** is the state of today: **How much money is still outstanding?** with the aging buckets (not overdue yet, 1 to 30, 31 to 60, 61 to 90, and more than 90 days overdue), **Who has kept you waiting longest?**, and **How quickly do your clients pay?** with the average payment time beside it.
- **Gone quiet** lists customers who bought from you in the last three years and not in the past year, with their previous spend, the date of their last paid invoice, and quick actions to call or email each of them. When no one qualifies, the page says so and states the rule. It is campaign-shaped work, so it lives in a report rather than in the urgency-sorted dashboard briefing.

## Work & time

**Work & time** answers **Where does my time go?** with the logged hours for the period, the hours per customer and per project, the billable-versus-non-billable split, and your effective hourly rate. **Am I leaving money on the table?** names the hours not invoiced yet with a link that invoices them. **What does an hour of work earn, per client?** and **Does working harder pay off?** weigh your hours against what they bring in. The hours criterion card answers **Will I meet the hours criterion?** against the year's threshold: on track at this pace or not, and the hours a week that would get you there; when only a few hours are logged, the card says so instead of guessing. That meter counts only your own confirmed worked hours plus travel time, and planned hours count once you confirm them, which is narrower than the total logged hours on the page, so the two figures can differ. On a workspace with a team, the page splits what the whole team logged and what you logged yourself.

## Expenses

**Expenses** answers **Where is my money going?** with the period's costs against the same period last year over an equally long window: **Have my costs gone up?** (by how much, or that they stayed level, possibly with a different mix of categories), **How much of my revenue goes on costs?** (the cents per euro and what you keep of every euro, against last year; when the costs were higher than the revenue, the card says by how much), and **What were the biggest expenses?** (the largest costs with their share, and the categories that moved: up, down, new this year, or gone). Averages sit per completed month, with the empty months in between counted, the same average the chart uses. Amounts on this page are without the VAT you remit or get back, like in the profit and loss statement. When last year's figures could not be loaded, the page says so instead of quietly showing only today.

## Cash flow

**Cash flow** answers **What came in and what went out?** and has two lenses to switch between: **What happened** and **What's coming**.

**What happened** follows the actual money, including VAT, on the day it moved. Invoices from previous years count in the month they were paid. That is why these amounts differ from your revenue and costs, which sit without VAT. The year chart answers **How did my money do this year?** (or for a past year, how it did then): one bar per month of what you kept or fell short in total since 1 January, with months still running marked, a month in the red named with when you got back to positive, and a year without a single negative month said plainly. **Do I rely on one good month?** names the month that brought in the largest share. The monthly table answers **How did each month go?** with what came in, what went out, and the balance per month, plus the row of what is still to come: the open invoices with their count, and when one or more are late, that callout too. A note under the totals says what still comes off the top (VAT and income tax) and links to your taxes.

**What's coming** estimates the coming months from the past year, over a horizon of **3 months**, **6 months** or **12 months**. **What will I keep over the coming {n} months?** answers with the expected in and out, what is already locked in recurring and sent invoices (when nothing is locked, the card says it is entirely an estimate), and the share of invoices it expects to be paid, taken equal to last year. **Will I run short at some point?** names the month or months that go negative, or answers no. **Is any money overdue?** counts the past year's invoices that sit past their due date; that money counts as coming, and a reminder is what actually brings it in. The chart draws a dashed line between what happened and what is expected, with the estimated parts labelled.

## Reach

**Reach** answers **How do people find me?** about your two outward channels. The newsletter side answers **Do people read my newsletter?** with the open rate, the clicks per 100 emails sent measured over the newsletters you sent in the period, the subscriber count with new and unsubscribed beside it, and subscriber growth per month. When nothing was sent in this period, the page says so; when you never sent one, it offers to create the first. The website side answers **Do people find my website?** with your visitor, request and booking counts, and points at publishing while the site is not live yet.

## Filters

Open the **Filters** panel with the button at the end of the tab row. The panel only shows the switches the open page actually reads, so a filter that would change nothing is not offered:

- **Period**: choose the granularity (**Year**, **Quarter**, **Month**, **Week**, or **Day**), with a matching picker for the chosen granularity
- **Year**: the reporting year
- **Include open invoices**: on the overview, the sales page and the buyers lens under Clients; the other pages decide this themselves

There is no compare switch anymore: the pages that compare always do so with the same period of last year, and their sentences say last year. The comparison window is always exactly as long as the period you picked.

## Tax estimates

The **What should I set aside?** card on the overview carries the summary. The full estimates live under **Taxes** in the app. The income tax sheet breaks the year down from gross profit through the self-employed deduction and SME profit exemption to taxable income, the tax brackets, estimated tax, profit after tax and how much to set aside, with the hours criterion, the assumptions behind the estimate and the small-business investment deduction (KIA) along the side. Selling an investment within five years of buying it can bring part of that deduction back through the desinvestment addition; MyCompanyDesk does not calculate that automatically, so check the amount yourself or with your accountant. When your company is a BV, the page estimates corporate tax instead. The Box 3 tab covers rented-out real estate with the total capital, the taxable base and the notional return; with multiple partners each partner splits the base and the notional return proportionally for their own Box 3 filing, and the amounts shown are the workspace total. Treat every estimate as a planning aid for reserving money, not as a filing.

VAT filing has its own dedicated page with quarterly overviews and filing support. See [VAT Management](/en/features/vat).

## Accounting view

The general ledger, balance sheet, profit-and-loss account and annual accounts live under **Accounting** in the sidebar, a separate section for the books, including write actions such as closing a year. Reports themselves stay analytics; the full accounting view is useful when you work with an accountant, and you do not need it for your own tax return.

The general ledger shows each ledger account with its RGS reference code where applicable. RGS (Referentie Grootboekschema) is the Dutch standard chart of accounts for entrepreneurs; the codes make it easier for you and your accountant to line up ledger lines with accounting software that supports RGS.

### Auditfile export (XAF)

Under Accounting, open the annual accounts for a year and choose **Auditfile (XAF)** to download a Dutch XML Auditfile Financieel 4.0 file. This file contains the same year-end ledger data as the PDF annual accounts, but in a structured format that accounting packages and the Dutch tax office (Belastingdienst) can import directly. It is useful when your accountant asks for the books in XAF format, or when you file electronically.

## Tips

- Follow the **Who still owes me money?** card on the overview and open the overdue invoices from there
- Look at the **Gone quiet** tab each quarter to win back previous customers
- Sales and Expenses always compare the same period last year; read their questions to see whether growth comes from one client
- Read **What will I keep over the coming months** before making commitments that cost money
- Check the **Will I meet the hours criterion?** card well before the year ends, so there is time to log your hours
